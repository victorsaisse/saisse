# Selling the work and running the studio

Building a launch video takes an evening now. Selling and delivering four a month is a different job. Sources: [beamnxw, motion engineering](https://x.com/beamnxw/status/2108616740846420354), [beamnxw, AI-employee studio](https://x.com/beamnxw/status/2111079523089322438) (a paid partnership post; the workflow works with any agent that has email, calendar, Drive and Slack access), [Dumble, platform rules](https://x.com/Y0urDumble/status/2115164938884862375).

## 1. The first product: a launch clip

Keep the first package small and concrete:

- One feature, one approved script.
- One 20-second video, 1080x1080 (add 9:16 as an extra).
- One cover image taken from an approved frame.
- One revision round covering timing, copy and sound, inside the approved script and direction.
- Extra formats, extra scenes and project source files are quoted separately.
- Illustrative pilot price from the source: $150. Test it against your real hours.

Before offering it, build one complete sample with your own product (or one whose owner agreed). Show the export next to its opening, action and closing stills. Time the whole job: assets, feedback, rendering, delivery.

Finding buyers: pick developers with a working feature and a release date. Name the one action their audience should understand ("your export feature could be the whole 20-second clip"), share the sample, ask which feature they want to show.

Agree before starting: script, assets, deadline, payment terms, revision scope, where the client may use the video, whether you may show it in your portfolio.

During production send, in order: three stills for direction, the moving preview, the final export. Keep every piece of feedback tied to frame numbers so the included revision has a clear list.

## 2. The studio around the videos

Seven recurring jobs. An agent drafts each one; a human approves every outbound step. Nothing reaches a client without a yes.

| Job | The agent does | You approve |
|---|---|---|
| Inquiry | Qualify: product live? screens ready? real deadline? budget? Draft a reply, propose two call times | the reply |
| Brief | Turn call notes into a production brief: product action, shots, copy, items to confirm before build | the brief |
| Quote | Pull two options from the rate card, draft the quote, hold it | which option goes out |
| Assets | Check logo, product screens, fonts, colours; list what is missing; block the build until complete | the asset list |
| Revisions | Log each client note with a timestamp, group notes into one round, flag anything that is really a new scene as a new quote | the round |
| Delivery and invoice | Send final files and invoice the same day, follow the payment | the send |
| Monday report | Pipeline, invoiced but unpaid, past due: one message by 9:00 | (read it) |

Give the agent a one-time onboarding message pointing at an operating file that defines how clients, projects and statuses are named, and tell it to read that file before answering anything about the studio.

## 3. Licences and rights

- Remotion: free for individuals and companies with up to 3 employees; a company licence from 4 employees. Check before client work at a company.
- OpenMontage is AGPL-3.0. motionmaxxing bundles GSAP under GreenSock's licence. GSAP itself and its plugins are free for commercial use.
- Poly Haven assets are CC0. earshot's bank is code-made. ElevenLabs music is cleared for most commercial uses depending on the plan.
- Never ship a competitor's footage, characters, music or exact scripts. Take the grammar (framing, pacing, caption style), never the content.

## 4. Channels and platforms

- Original pieces, not templates. YouTube's monetisation rules target mass-produced, repetitive content; a real story, a real voice and new visuals each time keep you clear.
- Label realistic AI content. Clearly unrealistic animation is generally exempt from YouTube's disclosure, but anything that could pass for real footage needs the label.
- Facts stay facts. One wrong date in the comments costs more trust than any render buys back.
- Learn from the audience-retention graph, not the view count: find the exact second people leave and fix that kind of shot next time.
- Make one piece end to end before planning a channel or a package. The second one tells you what the first really cost.
